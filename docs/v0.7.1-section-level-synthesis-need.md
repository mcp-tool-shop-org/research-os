## Title

Add section-level synthesis for gate-eligible sections in `repair_required` packs.

## Problem statement

Pack-level synthesis is all-or-nothing. A pack can contain one deliberately failed section preserved as evidence (e.g., a discover-hallucination containment artifact) and one gate-eligible curated section with accepted claims, but `research-os` cannot synthesize the eligible section without removing the failed section from `research.yaml` or completing unrelated sections.

This blocks useful partial research artifacts and forces operators to choose between evidence preservation and synthesis. Evidence preservation is the entire point of `research-os` — the product cannot ask operators to delete evidence in order to produce prose.

The fresh-pack proof exposed this concretely:

- Section 01 (`01-evidence-custody`) preserved as discover-hallucination evidence: 0 accepted claims, `accepted_claim_floor` failed (correctly — unwaiveable).
- Section 06 (`06-evidence-custody-curated`) advanced via curated URLs + source-card override + publisher-diversity waiver: 47 accepted claims, gate verdict WARN, `synthesis_eligible: true`.
- Cowork handoff: pack mode `repair_required`, synthesis allowed: false. Refused at `synth workspace`.

There is no operator path to synthesize section 06 alone. The product forces a contradiction between its stated thesis ("no synthesis without source truth, including preserved failed-section evidence") and its operator-facing behavior ("synthesis requires whole-pack readiness").

## Required semantics

```
research-os synth section <section-id>
```

or:

```
research-os synth workspace --section <section-id>
```

## Output contract

```yaml
status: partial_synthesis
scope: section
section_id: <id>
pack_mode: repair_required
not_freezable_as_pack: true
not_publishable_as_pack: true
```

## Rules

- Only allowed if the target section is gate-eligible (verdict pass or warn with waivers, `synthesis_eligible: true`).
- Does NOT imply pack-level synthesis eligibility.
- Does NOT alter failed sections.
- Does NOT make the pack freezable.
- Does NOT make the pack publishable.
- Must clearly mark the synthesis as section-scoped and provisional in the output artifact.
- Must preserve links to accepted claims, source cards, review decisions, waivers, and gate result.
- Output goes to a clearly-named section-scoped location (e.g., `sections/<id>/synthesis/`), not the pack-level `synthesis/` directory.

## Preserved laws

- Pack freeze remains all-or-nothing across sections.
- Pack publish remains all-or-nothing across sections.
- Section synthesis can exist as a working artifact but is not pack-level truth.
- Failed sections stay in the pack as evidence; no operator action required to "clean up" before partial synthesis.
- The accepted-claim floor remains unwaiveable for any section claiming synthesis-eligibility.

## Why it matters

Evidence preservation is the point of `research-os`. Failed sections can be evidence — in fact, the most important kind of evidence in a product proof. The fresh-pack proof depended on section 01's failure being preserved AND section 06's success being synthesizable. The product currently allows only one of those at a time.

Without section-level synthesis, the v1 product question — "does research-os produce a better artifact than a cloud LLM" — cannot be honestly answered for any pack where evidence preservation matters more than synthesis convenience. And evidence preservation is the entire reason the product exists.

## Adjacent v0.7.1 work — Local LLM Role Contract

Section-level synthesis is the headline. The fresh-pack proof also surfaced an architectural concern that section-level synthesis alone does not address: **local LLMs are currently being used as mini cloud-oracles inside an evidence machine, instead of as bounded interpreters over already-owned evidence.**

This is a broader redesign called the **Local LLM Role Contract**. Section-level synthesis is one deliverable within it. The contract:

> LLMs may propose. LLMs may extract. LLMs may classify. LLMs may critique. LLMs may synthesize from accepted evidence.
>
> LLMs may NOT create source truth. LLMs may NOT make a pack ready. LLMs may NOT override gates. LLMs may NOT turn unfetched leads into evidence.

### Deliverables under the Local LLM Role Contract

1. **Section-level synthesis** (this ticket). Provisional, section-scoped, not freezable.

2. **Discover demoted to lead generation only.** Today the `discover` subcommand uses the local LLM to propose URL candidates with titles and topic blurbs — and the LLM hallucinated 5 fabricated paper-title-to-arxiv-ID pairings in the fresh-pack proof. The output should be renamed `lead_candidates`, not source candidates. A lead becomes a source only after `gather` produces a fetch receipt + source card.

3. **Extraction becomes section-relevance-aware.** Currently the extractor answers "what claims are in this source?" It should answer "what claims in this source are relevant to this section question? Which are off-topic? Which are source chrome? Which are background-only?" Output fields: `relevance_to_section`, `off_topic_reason`, `chrome_or_content`.

4. **Review gains semantic section-alignment.** Today review catches structural issues (scope-widening, source dominance, claim overproduction) but misses "true but off-topic" cases. Section 01's containment was incidental to source-dominance, not semantic. Review should explicitly ask: "Does this claim support the section purpose? Is the source actually about the section topic? Is this claim useful for synthesis, or merely true?"

5. **Rerun the fresh pack from the same evidence question** once 1–4 land, to see if the artifact improves.

### Adjacent product fixes (smaller, not part of the role contract)

These were also surfaced by the fresh-pack proof. Each is a single-PR fix:

- **Gate publisher-diversity must use `getEffectivePublisher`, not `raw_publisher`.** Today `min_independent_publishers` reads raw metadata and ignores the source-card override ledger. The override path works at the audit layer but not at the gate layer.
- **Source-card override handbook schema is incomplete.** `site/src/content/docs/handbook/source-card-audit.md` shows 3 required override fields; the validator requires 7 (`source_id`, `url`, `operator`, `created_at`, `pack_version`, `reason`, plus at least one of `new_source_type` / `new_publisher`).
- **Section ID pattern is undocumented in `section add --help`.** The CLI enforces `^[0-9]{2}-[a-z0-9-]+$` but the help text doesn't say so; first two attempts at adding the curated companion section failed on this.

## What this ticket is NOT

- Not "rebuild the control plane." The pack layout, source cards, fetch receipts, claim ledgers, review ledgers, gate receipts, waiver discipline, freeze/publish verification, and append-only correction model all held under fresh-pack stress. They are the valuable product laws and should be preserved.
- Not "switch to cloud LLMs." The local-first thesis is sound; it's the LLM role contract that needs sharpening.
- Not "lower the bars." `accepted_claim_floor` doing its job is what made section 01 fail decisively — that's a feature, not a bug. Partial synthesis must not weaken it.
- Not v1.0 readiness. v1.0 readiness requires a successful end-to-end run that this section-level synthesis would enable. After v0.7.1 lands, the v1 question can be tested honestly.

## Acceptance criteria

1. `research-os synth section <section-id>` (or equivalent) produces a synthesis artifact for a gate-eligible section while the pack is in `repair_required` mode due to other sections.
2. The produced artifact is clearly marked as section-scoped, partial, and non-freezable.
3. Failed and unrun sections in the same pack are unaffected.
4. `pack freeze` and `pack publish` continue to refuse the pack as a whole until all sections are ready.
5. The fresh pack at `E:/AI/local-first-vs-cloud-research/` can produce a section-06 synthesis without modifying `research.yaml` or removing section 01.
6. Regression tests cover: (a) section-level synthesis on a clean section, (b) section-level synthesis on a section with active waivers, (c) section-level synthesis refused when the target section is not gate-eligible, (d) freeze/publish refusal preserved with partial synthesis present.
